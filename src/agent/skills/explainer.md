# Explainer and timeline playbook

One of the most-viewed AI-made videos of 2026 (3.9M views) was an illustrated AI-history timeline, and explainers are the second most-built category of video skills. What made them work:
- a strong visual style (doodle, origami, sand, paper, chalkboard);
- one idea per scene and a clear story arc;
- consistency across chapters.

What sank them: invented dates and slide-deck layouts.

## Plan before building
1. **Script:** a hook, 3–5 chapters and a synthesis. Ask about the audience and target length.
2. **Storyboard:** for each scene, the title, seconds, visual, voiceover line and on-screen text.
3. **Style bible:** palette, type, texture, motion rules and illustration style, kept in the storyboard brief and reused in every scene.
4. **Fact pass:** check every date, number, name and quote against the user's sources. Tell the user about anything you could not verify.

## Format
- 16:9 1920×1080, 30fps, 60–180s; a 9:16 cut of 45–60s via the shorts-captions skill.
- Voiceover at about 150 wpm (2.5 words/s).
- Scene length = measured voiceover duration + 0.3–0.6s. Never guess.

## Timeline (90s)
| Time | Beat | Build |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | Hook | A question or a surprising number: a counter or kinetic-title at 120px+, motion on frame 0. |
| 5–15s | Setup | Why it matters, with one diagram. |
| 15–70s | 3–5 chapters | A chapter-card (1.5–2s), then 10–15s per idea. Something changes every 3–5s: a new element, a camera move or a cut. |
| 70–85s | Synthesis | 3 takeaways, line stagger 6–8f, rise 15f, smooth. |
| 85–90s | Outro | An end-card or subscribe-cta. |

## History and timeline mode
- Lay the events out on one long horizontal canvas. Move the camera between events (keyframed position, 24–36f, smooth easing), then hold.
- Each event:
  - the year as a kinetic-title or counter, 120–160px;
  - a title at 56–64px and one line at 40–44px;
  - an illustration or archival image with a ken-burns loop.
- 4–8s per event. Show progress with a progress-bar or an era band. Use dip-to-black only between eras.

## Visual devices
- Diagrams built from shapes that draw on (draw progress 0→1 over 20–30f) with arrows, one element at a time.
- arrow-callout, circle-highlight and highlighter-sweep on key terms, 1–2 per scene.
- split-screen for comparisons, quote-card for quotes, map-route for geography, paper-texture for illustrated looks.
- In landscape, use 2–5 word key-term callouts instead of transcribing the voiceover; full captions only for social cuts.

## Motion and type
- Entrances 15–18f, smooth; staggers 3f for words, 6–8f for lines.
- One display face plus one body face.
- Body ≥40px, labels ≥32px, ≤12 words on screen.

## Sound
- Voiceover at −16 to −14 LUFS, with the music bed ducked 15–18 dB under the voice.
- Swell the music into chapter cards; light SFX on reveals (pop, paper rustle, chalk).
- Leave 0.3–0.5s of breathing room after big ideas.

## Banned
- Unverified facts or invented quotes.
- Title-plus-bullets slides; more than one idea per scene; more than 12 words on screen.
- Reading the full voiceover on screen in landscape.
- Mismatched illustration styles between chapters; generic stock montages.
- Centered gradient title cards; a camera that never stops moving.

## Self-check before finishing
- [ ] Every fact is traced to a source, and uncertain ones are flagged to the user.
- [ ] Each scene's length comes from its measured voiceover.
- [ ] A visual change every 3–5s; no static stretch longer than 5s.
- [ ] The style bible is applied in every chapter.
- [ ] The hook lands within 5s; the outro gives a clear next step.
- [ ] Ran the self-review skill.
